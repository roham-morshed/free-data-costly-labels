## 0. Experimental setup (numbers for the Setup section)

- **40 UCR datasets**, univariate, equal length, no missing values, `n_classes` ≤ 10, every class with ≥ 25 training instances.
- Label budgets **k ∈ [1, 2, 5, 10, 20]** labels per class; **10 seeds** (0–9).
- **8 seed-matched methods**: `1nn_dtw`, `sup_strict`, `sup_tf`, `self_train`, `label_spread`, `augment`, `safe_ssl`, `safe_ssl_novalve`, plus `sup_full` (the fully-supervised ceiling) computed once per dataset.
- **2000 cells** = 40 × 5 × 10, **16000 seed-matched result rows** plus **600** rows from the optional `deep_ssl` arm (3 seeds), **0 failures**.
- Feature transform: MiniRocket, `n_kernels = 10000` (feature dimensionality 9 996), one transform seed; feature cache **11.6 GB**.
- 1NN-DTW uses a Sakoe-Chiba band of 10% of the series length.
- Hardware: 16 logical CPUs, Windows-10-10.0.19045-SP0; CPU-only, one BLAS thread per worker.
- Software: python 3.11.4, aeon 1.5.0, scikit-learn 1.8.0, numpy 2.3.5, scipy 1.17.1.
- Total measured compute in the result rows: **22.45 CPU-hours**.

## 1. The four sentences the abstract can make

1. **Free unlabeled *features* are worthless.** Fitting the random-kernel transform on the unlabeled pool instead of on the labeled subset changes accuracy by **+0.01 points** on average over 40 datasets and 5 budgets, and is not significant at any budget (0/5 Wilcoxon tests with p < 0.05).
2. **Free unlabeled *labels* are worth something.** Pseudo-labeling on top of the same features adds **+1.17 points** on average (5/5 budgets positive).
3. **The gain is predictable without labels — with the sign reversed.** Spearman ρ between the Label-Free Alignment statistic and the realised gain is **negative at every budget** (-0.45 to -0.22, significant at 4/5); controlling for headroom makes it stronger (-0.48 to -0.29). SSL helps most where the unlabeled geometry is *worst*.
4. **A labeled-data-only safety valve removes most of the downside.** At k ≥ 2 the valve cuts the regression rate from **30.56%** to **9.94%** of (dataset, seed) pairs and the worst single loss from **35.54** to **18.01** accuracy points.

## 10. Verdict — what the paper must claim

- **Tier 1 (free unsupervised feature fitting) does not pay.** sup-tf beat sup-strict at 3/5 budgets, by +0.01 accuracy points on average, and the difference was significant (Wilcoxon, p<0.05) at 0/5 budgets. Fitting MiniRocket on the unlabeled pool instead of on the labeled subset is free, and it is also worth essentially nothing.
- **Tier 2 (pseudo-labeling) is where the benefit is.** SAFE-SSL beat sup-tf by +1.17 accuracy points on average (5/5 budgets positive, significant at 2/5).
- **THE LFA HYPOTHESIS IS REJECTED, WITH THE SIGN REVERSED.** The paper hypothesised that unlabeled data helps when the label-free geometry is already aligned with the class structure, i.e. a POSITIVE correlation between LFA and the SSL gain. Measured: Spearman rho is NEGATIVE at every budget (-0.45 to -0.22), significant at p<0.05 for 4/5 budgets. Semi-supervised learning helps MOST where the unlabeled feature geometry is WORST.
- The obvious confound -- that well-clustered datasets are already solved and so have no headroom -- does not explain it: LFA correlates with headroom at p<0.05 for only 0/5 budgets, and the partial correlation of LFA with the gain controlling for headroom is -0.48 to -0.29, i.e. as strong or stronger than the raw correlation.
- LFA is therefore still a usable free decision statistic -- it predicts the gain with |rho| up to 0.45 without touching a single label -- but the mechanism claimed in the hypothesis is wrong, and the paper must invert it.
- **The safety valve is what SAFE-SSL actually buys.** Averaged over the budgets where it can run (k>=2), it cuts the regression rate by 20.6 percentage points and the worst-case single loss from 35.5 to 18.0 accuracy points.
